03

Three pillars, one picture

The structure follows the original 2020 concept: how people are doing, whether care is there when they need it, and the conditions that shape where a country is heading.

Outcomes

Suicide, depression and anxiety, alcohol and drug harm, life evaluation.

Care

Psychiatrists and the wider workforce, psychiatric beds, public spending, treatment coverage, policy and law.

Conditions

Life satisfaction, social support and loneliness, unemployment, and how each outcome is changing over three years.

04

Nothing open does this yet

Existing products are surveys of self-selected samples, dashboards without a score, or one-off reports. None combines statistics-based scoring, broad coverage, a regular refresh, open licences and reproducible code.

Built only from open, official data

Every number is pulled by script from a public API and stored untouched, so any figure can be traced back to its source file. Sources whose licences do not allow reuse are left out until permission is in place.

Honest about uncertainty

When scores are published, each country will carry a rank interval from a sensitivity analysis, not a single position. Gaps and imputed values are flagged.

Fast where the data allows

Most inputs are annual and two to three years old. A quarterly pulse tracks the few series that move within the year, currently England and the United States.

Open to scrutiny

Method, code and changelog are versioned. An external statistical audit before the first ranking is part of the proposed design. See independence.
